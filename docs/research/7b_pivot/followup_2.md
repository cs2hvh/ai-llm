# FOLLOWUP: Tokenizer decision (three-way contradiction) plus sovereign-language data sufficiency for the multilingual differentiator

Three dimensions give three incompatible tokenizer directions and the choice is irreversible once pretraining starts: keep the existing 131k SPM-Unigram vocab (called the model's 'main remaining sovereign advantage'); retrain a new ~131-150k byte-level BPE on a code-heavy agentic mix; or adopt Qwen3's tokenizer outright to unlock drop-in logit distillation. These cannot all be satisfied, and the tokenizer cascades into distillation feasibility, code/JSON/shell inference cost, multilingual quality, and vLLM tool-call parser compatibility. Compounding this, the data plan devotes only ~5-10% of tokens to the sovereign languages that justify the entire 'sovereign model' identity, and no dimension quantified whether enough high-quality, commercially-licensable open data even exists in those languages or how much a 7B needs. Vocab allocation and language data mix must be decided together; getting either wrong either guts the differentiator or imposes a permanent per-token tax on the agentic workload.

## RECOMMENDATION
Retrain a NEW ~128k-152k byte-level BPE, and treat the sovereign advantage as a *data-mix property to reproduce*, not a file to preserve. Concretely: fit a tiktoken-style byte-level BPE (GPT-2/regex pretokenization, digit splitting, byte fallback, NFC-only normalization, a reserved 256-512 special-token block for chat/think/tool/FIM tags) on a mixture that is deliberately three-way balanced — a heavy code+JSON+shell+markup slice (to fix agentic fertility, the whole point of the pivot), a large English/web slice, AND an explicit Indic slice drawn from Sangraha-verified + HPLT so the new vocab keeps the 1.4-2.1 Indic fertility that is the real sovereign edge. This captures the byte-BPE camp's engineering, preserves the "keep 131k" camp's Indic efficiency (which is portable), and rejects only the literal code-naive file. Do NOT adopt Qwen3's tokenizer: cross-tokenizer distillation (ALM/ULD) is now near-lossless for subword targets, so keeping your own vocab no longer blocks distilling from Qwen3/Gemma/OLMo teachers, and adopting Qwen3's 119-language 151k vocab would import a thin Indic allocation and forfeit the sovereign identity for a distillation benefit you can mostly get anyway. Vocab ~128-152k, not Gemma's 262k: at 7B a tied 262k embedding is ~15% of params (untied ~30%) versus ~7-9% for 128-152k — the larger vocab is a compute tax a solo project should not pay. Size it once and freeze before pretraining. For the language mix, reject the 5-10%-across-~10-languages plan: minimal cross-family transfer means that split gives ~0.5-1% per language, below useful quality. Instead CONCENTRATE — pick a Tier-1 of 3-5 highest-data Indian languages (Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi; optionally add Kannada/Malayalam/Gujarati/Urdu as Tier-2), and raise the sovereign share to ~12-18% of pretraining tokens if the sovereign identity is genuinely load-bearing (or explicitly downgrade the claim to "light multilingual" if English+code+agentic is the true priority — do not straddle). Source that share from Sangraha-verified (CC-BY-4.0) + HPLT (CC0) + FineWeb-2 (ODC-BY) + MADLAD (CC-BY) at ~1.5-2.5 epochs on the highest-quality Indic; avoid CulturaX/mC4 as a primary source given its Common-Crawl-derived license for a DPDP/MeitY-clean posture. The tokenizer vocab allocation and the language token budget must be frozen together in one decision: allocate Indic vocab in rough proportion to the Tier-1/Tier-2 token budget you commit to, so fertility and data reinforce rather than contradict each other.

## FULL REPORT
## Tokenizer + sovereign-language-mix decision for a ~7B dense agentic model (from scratch, B200/B300)

*All facts below were verified live on 2026-07-23 via primary sources (HuggingFace config/tokenizer files, dataset cards, arXiv). Items I could not confirm live are marked UNVERIFIED.*

### 1. What the 2025-2026 flagships actually use (settles the "Unigram vs BPE" question)

Every current open flagship except Google's Gemma line is **byte-level BPE**, and even Gemma bolts byte-level encoding + digit splitting onto its SentencePiece:

| Model | Tokenizer type | Vocab | Source (fetched 2026-07-23) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B | byte-level BPE (Qwen2Tokenizer, tiktoken-style) | config `vocab_size` 151,936 (base ≈151,669; `</think>`=151,668) | https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B/blob/main/config.json |
| Llama 4 Scout | BPE | 202,048 | https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct/blob/main/config.json |
| gpt-oss-20b | BPE (o200k_harmony) | 201,088 | https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-20b/blob/main/config.json |
| OLMo 3 7B | BPE (`dolma2-tokenizer`, GPT2Tokenizer / cl100k-style) | 100,278 | https://huggingface.co/allenai/Olmo-3-1025-7B/blob/main/config.json + https://huggingface.co/allenai/dolma2-tokenizer/blob/main/tokenizer_config.json |

Takeaways: (a) **Unigram-only is now a minority of one** (Gemma), and Gemma's own report calls its tokenizer "more balanced for non-English languages" only because it added byte-level encoding and digit splitting — the *pretokenization + byte-fallback config* matters more than the Unigram-vs-BPE label. (b) The efficient vocab band for a 7B is **~100k-152k** (OLMo 100k, Qwen 152k); 202k-262k is chosen by labs that can absorb the embedding-parameter cost. For a 7B at hidden≈4096, a tied 262k embedding is ~1.07B params (~15% of the model) and untied ~2.15B (~30%); a tied 128-152k embedding is ~0.52-0.62B (~7-9%). This alone argues against copying Gemma's 262k for a compute-lean solo project.

### 2. Code / JSON / shell fertility: it's the *training mix and pretokenization*, not the algorithm

From "Getting the most out of your tokenizer" (arXiv 2402.01035, Feb 2024, https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01035v2): on Python, a GPT-4-style 256k BPE compresses ~26% better than the Llama tokenizer; but **vocab size itself has near-zero correlation with code accuracy** (r=-0.13, p=0.87 vs HumanEval Pass@1). Crucially, *over-compression hurts*: their max-compression "Identity 100k" tokenizer got 30% better compression yet **dropped HumanEval Pass@1 to 20.5% vs 22.8%**. Their recommendation is a GPT-4-class BPE with digit splitting and byte fallback — i.e., exactly the "tiktoken-style pretokenization + digit splitting + byte fallback" the retrain proposal specifies.

The decisive fact for this project: **the existing 131k SPM-Unigram was fit on a *general multilingual* 13-source corpus with no code.** A tokenizer never exposed to `{`, `}`, indentation runs, `const`, `def`, `->`, `</tool_call>`, or JSON punctuation at training time will fragment code/JSON/shell regardless of whether it is Unigram or BPE — every mis-tokenized brace and 4-space indent becomes a permanent per-token tax on the workload the model is *supposed to be best at*. Exact bytes/token for the current 131k tokenizer on Python/JS/shell/JSON is UNVERIFIED (project-internal), but the structural conclusion is robust: a code-naive vocab is disqualifying for an agentic model.

On the flip side, the Indic efficiency that the "keep it" camp calls the sovereign advantage is real and measurable: Sarvam-1 (https://huggingface.co/sarvamai/sarvam-1, Oct 2024) reports **fertility 1.4-2.1 tokens/word on Indian languages, 2-4x more efficient than existing multilingual tokenizers.** That advantage comes from *allocating vocab to Indic scripts using Indic data* — it is a property of the training mix, not of the specific 131k file. It is fully reproducible in a new tokenizer.

### 3. Data availability for sovereign / non-English languages (is there enough?)

There is substantially more commercially-licensable Indic data than the 5-10% plan assumes — enough to support a much larger fraction *if concentrated*:

- **AI4Bharat Sangraha** (https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/sangraha, CC-BY-4.0): 251.3B tokens across 22 Indian languages; **64.3B human-verified**, 162.7B synthetic (English Wikimedia translated), 24.3B unverified. Verified per-language (millions of tokens): Hindi 12,617 / Bengali 10,604 / Tamil 3,985 / Telugu 3,707 / Gujarati 3,648 / Urdu 3,658 / Marathi 2,827 / Malayalam 2,731 / Nepali 1,823 / Kannada 1,778. **This is the highest-quality, cleanest commercial Indic license available.**
- **HPLT 3.0** (https://hplt-project.org/datasets/v3.0, **CC0**): web tokens — Hindi 26.77B, Bengali 16.36B, Malayalam 6.64B, Telugu 5.75B, Marathi 4.68B, Kannada 3.91B, Tamil 3.37B, Gujarati 3.33B. ~70B+ across major Indic, no attribution constraint.
- **FineWeb-2** (https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-2, **ODC-BY**): >1T tokens across 1,000+ language-script configs including all major Indic scripts (per-language Indic counts UNVERIFIED from the card, but coverage confirmed).
- **MADLAD-400** (https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/MADLAD-400, CC-BY-4.0): 419 languages; clean Indic e.g. Hindi 3.8B, Marathi 776.9M, Bengali 645.7M, Telugu 618.5M, Tamil 1.1B tokens. (Its total-token figure returned by the card, ~11.4T, looks inflated vs the commonly cited ~3T and is UNVERIFIED.)
- **CulturaX** (https://huggingface.co/datasets/uonlp/CulturaX): 6.3T tokens / 167 languages; Hindi 16.79B, Urdu 2.70B, Bengali 9.57B, Malayalam 2.10B, etc. **License caveat:** it inherits mC4 + OSCAR terms (Common-Crawl-derived); treat as higher-risk for a "sovereign / DPDP / MeitY-clean" posture and prefer Sangraha/HPLT.
- **Nemotron-CC-v2** (https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-CC-v2, NVIDIA Data Agreement, Aug 2025): 6.6T tokens, English-dominant, only "Translated Diverse QA" into 15 languages (~558B) — not an Indic backbone.

**Sufficiency math.** A modern (overtrained) 7B commonly sees ~1.5-3T tokens. At a 12-15% sovereign share that is ~200-450B Indic tokens. The genuinely commercial, cleanly-licensed pool (Sangraha verified 64B CC-BY + HPLT ~70B CC0 + FineWeb-2 Indic slice + MADLAD Indic ~10B) is roughly **150-200B before repetition**; adding Sangraha synthetic/translated (163B) and CulturaX (license-permitting) pushes past 350B. So a meaningful sovereign fraction is *achievable for the top 3-6 languages* at ~1.5-2.5 epochs, but is **not** achievable at high quality if spread thinly across 22 languages.

### 4. Minimum multilingual fraction + the transfer trap

The most decision-relevant study is "Scaling Laws for Multilingual Language Models" (arXiv 2410.12883, Oct 2024, https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12883v2): across 5 families (Germanic, Romance, Slavic, Indic, Sino-Tibetan) they find **minimal cross-family transfer — each family's test loss depends almost solely on its own sampling ratio.** Their *multilingual-optimal* Indic share is 29.8% (but that is for a model whose entire job is those 5 families). The load-bearing implication for an English+code-first agentic 7B: **the fraction you allocate to Indic is very close to the fraction that determines Indic quality; English/code tokens will not rescue it.** Therefore 5-10% split across ~10 Indian languages (~0.5-1% each) is below the useful threshold per language. Gemma 3/4's answer to imbalance is a large 262k vocab plus explicit up-weighting of monolingual+parallel multilingual data (arXiv 2503.19786); Qwen3 supports 119 languages with a 151k vocab (arXiv 2505.09388, May 2025) — both spend real vocab *and* real token budget, not a 5% afterthought. A precise universal "minimum %" is UNVERIFIED (no single number in the literature), but the concentration principle is well supported.

### 5. Cross-tokenizer distillation now works — which defuses the "adopt Qwen3's tokenizer" argument

The strongest reason offered for adopting Qwen3's 151k vocab was "drop-in logit distillation from Qwen3 teachers." That reason is now weak on two fronts:

- **Cross-tokenizer distillation is a solved-enough problem.** ALM / Approximate Likelihood Matching (arXiv 2503.20083, Mar 2025, https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20083v2): transferring Llama-3.2-3B to the Qwen2 tokenizer is *near lossless* (77.1 vs 76.9 original); even subword→**byte-level** transfer costs only ~2-3 points (74.3-74.7 vs 76.9), beating prior methods (DSKD, MinED). ULD (arXiv 2402.12030, TMLR Jan 2025) established optimal-transport logit distillation across different tokenizers.
- **Same-tokenizer distillation's *extra* edge is small.** In ALM's large→small cross-tokenizer math case (OpenMath-8B → Gemma-2-2B, different tokenizers), ALM+SFT reached 49.0 avg vs 47.7 SFT-only — a ~1.3-point gain, ~66% of teacher. So the incremental value that a *matching* tokenizer would add over plain SFT + modern cross-tokenizer KD is modest, not the decisive multiplier the "adopt Qwen3" case assumed.

Adopting Qwen3's vocab would also **import Qwen3's Indic allocation** (a 151k vocab covering 119 languages leaves relatively little headroom per Indic script), directly eroding the sovereign efficiency advantage — you'd pay the sovereign-identity cost to chase a distillation benefit you can largely get anyway.

### Synthesis

The three positions are reconcilable once you separate *what is actually valuable in each*:
- The "keep 131k" camp is right that **Indic token efficiency is the differentiator** — but wrong that the *specific file* is the asset; the asset is Indic-weighted vocab allocation, which is portable, and the current file is code-naive.
- The "retrain byte-level BPE" camp is right on **engineering** (byte-BPE, tiktoken pretokenization, digit splitting, byte fallback, reserved special-token block) — this is what every non-Gemma flagship does and what fixes code/JSON/shell.
- The "adopt Qwen3" camp's core premise (**distillation requires a matching tokenizer**) is now largely obsolete, and adoption sacrifices the sovereign advantage.

The winning move takes the engineering from camp 2, preserves the Indic efficiency from camp 1, and declines camp 3.
