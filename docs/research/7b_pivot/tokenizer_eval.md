# Tokenizer choice, eval suite, deployment + governance

## RECOMMENDATION
Retrain the tokenizer; do not carry the 131k SPM-Unigram into the 7B run unmeasured. The vocab was fit on a 13-source general multilingual mix, and an agentic model's traffic is dominated by code, JSON tool calls, and shell/markup — token distributions the old vocab underweights, imposing a permanent per-token tax on exactly the workload that matters. Train a ~131-150k byte-level BPE (HF tokenizers, tiktoken-style pretokenization: digit splitting, whitespace-run tokens, byte fallback) on the new code-heavy mix, and reserve a special-token block up front for chat markup, <think>, Hermes-style tool tags, and FIM — this is what every 2025-2026 agentic flagship (Qwen3, Llama 4, gpt-oss, OLMo 3) does, it makes vLLM's existing hermes/deepseek_r1 parsers work day-1, and it costs hours against a ~10^24-FLOP pretrain. Keep SPM-Unigram only if a fertility benchmark on Python/JS/shell/JSON shows it within ~5-10% of Qwen3's bytes/token; skip SuperBPE (real gains on paper, zero flagship adoption). Pair this with a plain GQA transformer + interleaved sliding-window attention (OLMo 3/Gemma 4 pattern): the Qwen3-Next episode shows hybrid linear attention costs ~2.5+ months of llama.cpp lag, while a standard decoder gets day-1 vLLM/SGLang/GGUF and the full FP8/NVFP4/Q4 quantization ladder down to 16GB consumer GPUs.

For evals, copy the OLMo 3 pattern: in-loop per-source perplexity plus an OlmoBaseEval-style easy suite at fixed steps, full base suite (MMLU/-Pro, GPQA-Diamond, BBEH, MATH-500, GSM8K, HumanEval+/MBPP+) at major checkpoints via lm-eval-harness/OLMES, and evalchemy post-train with the agentic core of IFEval, BFCL v4, tau2-bench, and AgentDojo. Safety post-training should be the boring open recipe — Tulu 3 safety mix (CoCoNot/WildJailbreak/WildGuardMix) in SFT+DPO, SecAlign++-style injection-resistance preference training, gpt-oss-safeguard-20b (Apache 2.0) as a runtime policy guard driven by the existing safety_policy.md — plus deterministic tool-action mediation outside the model. Governance is a strength to keep: the repo's model/data cards, license register, and EU AI Act/ISO 42001/MeitY scaffolding carry over wholesale; the 7B lands at ~2.5-6.3x10^23 FLOP, i.e., baseline GPAI transparency duties only (technical docs, copyright policy, public training-content summary on the official template) and only if placed on the EU market — an order of magnitude-plus below the 10^25 systemic-risk threshold. Priority additions from the existing Phase 5 backlog: eval card, risk card, incident response, and the OWASP Agentic Top 10 matrix, which an agentic release makes mandatory rather than optional.

Takeaways: (a) the 2026 mainstream band is **100k–262k**; the project's 131k is squarely inside it for a 7B (Qwen3's 151,936 on an 8B is the closest comparable). (b) **Every agentic/code-focused flagship (Qwen3, Llama 4, gpt-oss, OLMo 3, DeepSeek) uses byte-level BPE**; the only large SentencePiece holdout is the Gemma/Gemini line, which proves SPM is fully servable (Gemma 4 had day-1 llama.cpp/vLLM/Ollama support). (c) Universal hygiene: byte fallback / byte-level encoding, digit splitting (explicit in the [Gemma 4 report](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02770v1); individual-digit splitting in Qwen is UNVERIFIED live but widely documented), and preserved-whitespace handling so code indentation compresses well. (d) Modern tokenizers reserve a **special-token block for chat/tool/reasoning markup** — o200k_harmony added ~1,088 tokens over o200k specifically for the Harmony tool/channel format ([Modal, 2025](https://modal.com/blog/what-is-o200k-harmony)); Gemma 4 exposes `<|tool>` / `<|tool_call|>`-style special tokens ([tech report](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02770v1)).

### BPE vs Unigram evidence

Academic evidence mildly favors Unigram on morphology-rich languages: ["Rethinking Tokenization for Rich Morphology" (arXiv:2508.08424, IJCNLP 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08424) found Unigram consistently outperforming BPE in small-model settings, and the older ["Tokenizer Choice For LLM Training: Negligible or Crucial?" (arXiv:2310.08754)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.08754) showed tokenizer choice measurably moves 2.6B-scale downstream results. But no 2025-2026 production flagship switched to Unigram; the ecosystem's tooling, fertility benchmarks, and code-handling recipes (tiktoken-style pretokenizer regexes) are BPE-centric. Fertility varies wildly by language and is not predicted by vocab size alone — e.g., on Ukrainian, Llama 4 Maverick ≈2.43 tokens/word vs Qwen3 ≈3.90 ([arXiv:2605.14890](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.14890), 2026).

### SuperBPE and 2025-2026 innovations

[SuperBPE (arXiv:2503.13423)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13423) reports up to 33% fewer tokens at fixed vocab and +4.0% avg downstream at 8B scale, with a 128k English tokenizer update released 2025-11-03 ([project page](https://superbpe.github.io/)); follow-ons include BoundlessBPE/SupraTok ([EmergentMind survey](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/superword-tokenization-superbpe)). **Adoption check: none of Gemma 4, Qwen3, Llama 4, OLMo 3, or gpt-oss uses superword tokenization** (verified via the configs/reports above). For a "proven stack" project, SuperBPE remains a research bet, not a default.

### Reuse the 131k SPM-Unigram or retrain?

The existing tokenizer was fit on a 13-source multilingual corpus for a general 1B. The pivot changes the token distribution the model will live in: agentic trajectories are dominated by code, JSON tool calls, file paths, shell output, and markup. A vocab fit without heavy code weighting underallocates merges to indentation runs, camelCase identifiers, and brace-heavy JSON — a permanent per-token tax on exactly the traffic this model is for. Retraining a tokenizer costs hours; the vocab is the one decision you cannot patch after a ~10^24-FLOP pretrain. Recommendation details in the recommendation section.

## 2. Eval suite for a 2026 from-scratch effort

### Harnesses (maintenance status, July 2026)

- **lm-evaluation-harness (EleutherAI)** — still the de-facto standard; v0.4.12 (May 2026), 200+ tasks ([tutorial/status, 2026](https://qaskills.sh/blog/lm-evaluation-harness-tutorial-2026); [repo lineage paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.14782)).
- **OLMES (Ai2)** — reproducible-eval toolkit; its new **OlmoBaseEval** collection was used to develop OLMo 3 base, with a "Base Easy" suite as the small-scale proxy and "Base Main" for full-scale runs ([Ai2 blog, 2025-11-20](https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3); [Papers-Explained summary, 2026-06](https://ritvik19.medium.com/papers-explained-571-olmo-3-1ee7134a4e67)). This is the best public template for *in-training* eval of a from-scratch base model.
- **Evalchemy (mlfoundations / Bespoke Labs / DataComp)** — actively maintained (issues into Dec 2025), builds on lm-eval-harness, strongest for post-train benchmarks: AIME24/25, MATH500, LiveCodeBench, GPQA-Diamond, HumanEval+, MBPP+, BigCodeBench, MultiPL-E, CRUXEval, with multi-GPU sharding ([README](https://github.com/mlfoundations/evalchemy/blob/main/README.md)).
- **LightEval (HF)** — fine if HF-Hub-centric ([2026 tutorial](https://cohorte.co/blog/lighteval-deep-dive-hugging-faces-all-in-one-framework-for-llm-evaluation)); not required here.

### Base-model suite (checkpoint-time)

What Gemma 4 reports in 2026 is a good snapshot of the current canon: **MMLU-Pro, GPQA-Diamond, Big-Bench Extra Hard (BBEH — note BBH is being replaced), AIME 2026, LiveCodeBench, plus RULER/LOFT/GraphWalks/MTOB for long context** ([Gemma 4 report](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02770v1)). For a 7B base: MMLU + MMLU-Pro, GPQA-Diamond, BBH→BBEH, MATH-500 + GSM8K, HumanEval+/MBPP+ (EvalPlus variants, both in evalchemy), ARC/HellaSwag/PIQA-class easy tasks for early signal, and per-source held-out perplexity. OLMo's practice (in-loop perplexity + downstream proxies at fixed steps; OLMo-core added `fixed_steps` checkpoint/eval callbacks and in-loop PPL evals with TP/CP — [OLMo-core CHANGELOG](https://github.com/allenai/OLMo-core/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)) is the model to copy: cheap "easy suite" every ~1k steps, full Base Main suite at major checkpoints, per-source PPL continuously (Paloma-style per-source perplexity, per the original [OLMo paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.00838)).

### Instruct/agentic suite (post-training)

2026 consensus set ([benchmark surveys, 2026](https://benchmarkingagents.com/agent-benchmarks/); [decodethefuture 2026](https://decodethefuture.org/en/ai-agent-benchmarks-2026/)): **IFEval (instruction following), BFCL v4** (function calling incl. multi-turn, web search, memory — [awesomeagents leaderboard](https://awesomeagents.ai/leaderboards/agentic-ai-benchmarks-leaderboard/)), **tau2-bench** (tool-agent-user with policy adherence; 2026 update added voice + retrieval domains), **Terminal-Bench 1.0/2.0** (executable terminal workflows), **SWE-bench Verified** (aspirational at 7B; use Lite/subsets), GAIA, and **AgentDojo** for prompt-injection robustness (97 user tasks across workspace/banking/travel/slack domains — [Meta SecAlign paper, arXiv:2507.02735](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02735v1)). Safety-side: WildGuard-based harm/refusal scoring plus over-refusal checks (CoCoNot/XSTest-style), per the Tulu 3 methodology ([arXiv:2411.15124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124)).

## 3. Deployment targets that should shape design

### Architecture ↔ serving support

- **Plain GQA dense transformer (optionally with sliding-window/full attention interleave, Gemma/OLMo 3-style): day-1 everywhere.** Gemma 4 launched with coordinated day-1 support in Transformers, vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp, MLX, Ollama ([NVIDIA blog 2026](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/bringing-ai-closer-to-the-edge-and-on-device-with-gemma-4/); [AMD day-0 note, 2026](https://www.amd.com/en/developer/resources/technical-articles/2026/day-0-support-for-gemma-4-on-amd-processors-and-gpus.html)). OLMo 3's config confirms interleaved sliding-window + full attention + YaRN to 65k is a fully supported pattern ([config.json](https://huggingface.co/allenai/Olmo-3-1125-32B/raw/main/config.json)).
- **Hybrid linear-attention/SSM: months of llama.cpp lag, measured.** Qwen3-Next (hybrid Gated DeltaNet) shipped 2025-09-11; the llama.cpp feature request opened same day ([issue #15940](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/15940)); the implementing PR opened 2025-09-18 and **merged 2025-11-28 — ~2.5 months of hard porting work** (memory-pool issues, 16GB graph blowups for a 500M model, delta-net op restructuring) ([PR #16095](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/16095)). CUDA/Vulkan performance tuning was explicitly deferred past merge.
- **vLLM's Transformers backend** now runs any HF-compatible architecture at near-native speed with runtime layer fusion ([HF blog, 2026](https://huggingface.co/blog/native-speed-vllm-transformers-backend); [ODSC summary, 2026-06](https://odsc.medium.com/vllm-transformers-backend-bridging-hugging-face-compatibility-and-high-performance-inference-b7ef0d39f005)) — so shipping a clean HF `modeling_*.py` for a standard decoder buys near-day-1 vLLM/SGLang serving even without a native reimplementation.
- **Tool calling in serving is a format decision made at training time.** vLLM ships named tool-call parsers (`--tool-call-parser hermes` works for Qwen because Qwen's chat template embeds Hermes-style tool markup) and reasoning parsers (`--reasoning-parser deepseek_r1` for `<think>` blocks) ([vLLM tool-calling docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/features/tool_calling/); [Qwen function-call docs](https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/latest/framework/function_call.html)). Training on an already-parsed format (Hermes-style tool JSON + `<think>...</think>`) means zero custom parser work at deploy time.

### Quantization paths on Blackwell + consumer feasibility

- **FP8 serving** on B200/B300 is table stakes; **NVFP4** is the Blackwell-native 4-bit format: up to 3.13× throughput vs BF16 on B200 ([NVIDIA PTQ blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/optimizing-llms-for-performance-and-accuracy-with-post-training-quantization/)); TensorRT Model Optimizer exports NVFP4/FP8/INT8 checkpoints consumable by vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM.
- **Consumer feasibility of an agentic 7-8B is proven with numbers**: a Jan 2026 study ([arXiv:2601.09527](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09527v1)) benchmarked Qwen3-8B on consumer Blackwell GPUs — NVFP4 gives 1.6× throughput over BF16 with 2-4% quality loss; the 8B fits every tested 16GB card; 411 TPS on RTX 5090, 115 TPS on RTX 5060 Ti at 8k context. GGUF Q4 via llama.cpp adds CPU/Mac coverage (day-1 if the arch is standard). AWQ/W4A16 preserves quality with smaller speedups (+31%).

## 4. Safety + governance for an agentic model from a small company

### Minimal credible safety post-training (all-open recipe exists)

1. **Refusal/safety SFT+DPO**: the Tulu 3 recipe is fully open — safety mixture built from **CoCoNot (contrastive over-refusal control), WildJailbreak, WildGuardMix**; ablations show safety data is orthogonal to other skills ([Tulu 3, arXiv:2411.15124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124); [datasets](https://huggingface.co/collections/allenai/tulu-3-datasets)). The project's existing safety_policy.md (hard-refusal categories + comply-with-care + anti-jailbreak posture) maps directly onto this data curation step.
2. **Guard model at the boundary**: **gpt-oss-safeguard-20b** (Apache 2.0, 2025-10-29) reasons over a developer-supplied policy at inference — i.e., your safety_policy.md becomes the runtime policy without classifier retraining ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-oss-safeguard/)); alternatives: Qwen3Guard (2025-09, multilingual, [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/09/26/meet-qwen3guard-the-qwen3-based-multilingual-safety-guardrail-models-built-for-global-real-time-ai-safety/)), Llama Guard 4, WildGuard-7B.
3. **Prompt-injection resistance**: **Meta SecAlign / SecAlign++** is the public training recipe — preference optimization favoring the legitimate instruction over the injected one; it cut AgentDojo attack success from 14.1%→2.1% and InjecAgent from 53.8%→0.5% ([arXiv:2507.02735](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02735v1)). Note the 2024-2026 literature consensus: training helps, but robust agent security also needs **deterministic out-of-model mediation** of tool actions (allowlists, sandboxing, action policies) ([adaptive-evaluation survey, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26479)); OWASP Agentic Top 10 mitigation matrix is already on the project's Phase 5 list.
4. **Evaluate**: AgentDojo (injection), WildGuardMix-based harm scoring, over-refusal (CoCoNot held-out).

### EU AI Act position (as of 2026-07-23)

- GPAI obligations apply since **2025-08-02**; the Commission **fully enforces from 2026-08-02** — i.e., ten days from now ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-ai-act-gpai-model-obligations-in-force-and-final-gpai-code-of-practice-in-place); [EC digital-strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/eu-rules-general-purpose-ai-models-start-apply-bringing-more-transparency-safety-and-accountability)).
- **Thresholds**: Commission Guidelines (2025-07) presume a model is GPAI if training compute **>10^23 FLOP** and it generates language; **≥10^25 FLOP** presumes systemic risk with heavy Art. 55 duties ([Stibbe analysis](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/the-guidelines-for-providers-of-general-purpose-ai-models-are-here-the); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20250724-european-commission-issues-guidelines-for-providers-of-general-purpose-ai-models)).
- **This project's math**: 6ND for 7B × 6-15T tokens = **2.5×10^23 – 6.3×10^23 FLOP** (reaching 10^25 would require ~240T tokens at 7B — not plausible). So: **clearly a GPAI model (baseline Art. 53 duties), clearly NOT systemic-risk**. Duties if placed on the EU market: technical documentation, downstream-provider information, copyright policy, and a **public training-content summary using the official template (published 2025-07-24)** ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/eu-ai-act-news-rules-on-general-purpose-ai-start-applying-guidelines-and-template-for-summary-of-training-data-finalized)). A free, genuinely open-source release would exempt the documentation duties (Art. 53(2)) but **never** the copyright policy + training-data summary ([Slaughter & May](https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102kzax/what-do-the-open-source-exemptions-for-gpai-models-mean-for-you-the-eu-ai-act-gu); [HF explainer](https://huggingface.co/blog/yjernite/eu-act-os-guideai)). If the model is never placed on the EU market (internal/India-only), these duties do not attach — but the artifacts are cheap insurance.
- **Carryover from the old project is high**: the repo's `docs/governance/` (model card + data card with auto-render from configs, license register, EU AI Act/ISO 42001/NIST AI RMF/OWASP/MeitY mapping) and `docs/safety_policy.md` are architecture- and scale-agnostic; they carry over wholesale. Gaps (their own Phase 5 list, now more urgent for an *agentic* model): eval card, risk card, incident response, OWASP Agentic Top 10 matrix, and the formal Art. 53 technical-documentation packet. ## 5. Sub-recommendations (condensed)

- **Tokenizer**: retrain on the new agentic/code-heavy mix; target ~130-150k byte-level BPE with digit splitting, whitespace-run tokens, byte fallback, and a reserved special-token block (chat markup, `<think>`, Hermes-style tool tags, FIM triplet, ~64 spare slots). Reuse of the 131k SPM-Unigram is acceptable **only** if measured code fertility (bytes/token on Python/JS/shell/JSON) is within ~5-10% of Qwen3 — the vocab content (fit on a non-code-heavy mix) is the problem, more than the algorithm. Skip SuperBPE.
