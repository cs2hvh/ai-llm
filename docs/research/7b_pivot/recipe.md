# Optimizer + Pretraining Recipe Evidence at 7B Scale (research cutoff: 2026-07-23)

## 1. Muon at >=7B: the production evidence base

**Moonlight (Feb 2025).** Moonshot AI's "Muon is Scalable for LLM Training" ([arXiv:2502.16982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16982), Feb 2025) trained Moonlight, a 16B-total / 3B-active MoE, on 5.7T tokens with Muon. Two techniques were required to scale Muon: (a) decoupled weight decay on Muon-updated matrices, and (b) per-parameter **update-RMS matching** (rescaling each orthogonalized update so its RMS matches a target, letting AdamW-tuned LRs carry over). They self-report "~2x computational efficiency vs AdamW with compute-optimal training" — note this is the vendor's own number at small-scale scans, not an independent 7B head-to-head. Distributed Muon (memory-optimal, communication-efficient) is open-sourced ([github.com/MoonshotAI/Moonlight](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Moonlight)).

**Kimi K2 (Jul 2025) — the flagship proof.** K2 ([arXiv:2507.20534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534), Jul 2025) is a 1.04T-param / 32B-active MoE pretrained on **15.5T tokens with MuonClip = Muon + weight decay + update-RMS matching + QK-Clip**, reporting **zero loss spikes over the entire run**. Concrete recipe from the report ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20534v1)): QK-Clip threshold tau=100 (per-head rescaling of q/k projections when max attention logit exceeds tau; for MLA, q^C/k^C scaled by sqrt(gamma), q^R by gamma, shared k^R untouched); WSD schedule — 500-step warmup, constant LR 2e-4 for the first 10T tokens, cosine decay 2e-4 -> 2e-5 over the next 5.5T, final anneal 2e-5 -> 7e-6; weight decay 0.1; global batch 67M tokens; pretraining context 4,096.

**GLM-4.5 (Aug 2025).** Zhipu/Z.ai's GLM-4.5 ([arXiv:2508.06471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06471), Aug 2025), 355B-total / 32B-active MoE trained on 23T tokens, used **Muon for all parameters except embeddings, biases and RMSNorm weights**, citing faster convergence and tolerance of **larger batch sizes**; Muon update RMS scaled to **0.2**, momentum 0.95.

**Motif-2-12.7B (Nov 2025) — closest public *dense* ~7-13B evidence.** Motif Technologies' dense 12.7B ([arXiv:2511.07464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07464), Nov 2025) was pretrained on **5.5T tokens with MuonClip, in FP8, under FSDP with "Parallel Muon" kernels**, with a linear data-curriculum shifting from general web toward STEM/code. This is the strongest existence proof that MuonClip works at exactly the dense scale and token budget this project is targeting.

**2026 additions.** The Kimi lineage continued (Kimi Linear 48B-A3B, K2.x) on MuonClip. No public evidence of a Muon failure at scale surfaced; the main practical caveats reported in 2026 literature are numerical sensitivity of the Newton–Schulz iteration under reduced precision (mitigable; see e.g. quantized-Muon-state work, [arXiv:2509.23106](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.23106)) and the attention-logit explosion issue that QK-Clip was invented for.

## 2. Independent benchmarks: the honest speedup vs well-tuned AdamW

**Stanford/Marin "Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers I" ([arXiv:2509.02046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046), Sep 2025).** Ten optimizers, rigorously tuned baselines, 0.1B–1.2B params, 1x–16x Chinchilla. Findings: no optimizer achieves the claimed 2x; the best is **~1.4x step-wise speedup over AdamW at 0.1B, decaying to ~1.1x at 1.2B** under standard constant decoupled weight decay. Matrix-based optimizers (Muon, SOAP, Kron/PSGD) beat all scalar-based ones; **Muon wins in the 1x–4x Chinchilla regime, but SOAP/Kron overtake at >=8x–16x overtraining** — directly relevant because a 7B @ 6T tokens is ~43x Chinchilla.

**"Hyperparameter Transfer Enables Consistent Gains of Matrix-Preconditioned Optimizers Across Scales" ([arXiv:2512.05620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05620), Dec 2025).** Counter-result to the "gains shrink with scale" narrative: on Llama-style 190M–1.4B models, **Muon/SOAP/Shampoo hold a consistent ~1.4x speedup over AdamW when LR is scaled per muP and weight decay is scaled ~1/width**; the speedup "vanishes rapidly with scale under incorrect scaling." I.e., the shrinkage seen in Fantastic-I is largely a hyperparameter-scaling artifact, not a property of Muon.

**"Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers II: Hyperball" ([arXiv:2606.16899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16899), Jun 15 2026).** Same Stanford group (Wen, Dang, Lyu, Ma, Liang): replacing decoupled weight decay with **Hyperball** (fix Frobenius norms of weights and updates to constants) gives **Muon+Hyperball a 20–30% token-equivalent speedup over weight-decay baselines on Qwen3-style models up to 1.2B**, and **improves LR transfer across widths and depths** vs decoupled WD. Their framing: weight decay's equilibrium-norm dynamics are what erode Muon's edge at scale.

**Essential AI, "Practical Efficiency of Muon" ([arXiv:2505.02222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02222), May 2025).** Up to 4B params: Muon **expands the compute-time Pareto frontier over AdamW**, retaining data efficiency **far beyond the critical batch size** — the key systems argument for Muon on large clusters (bigger batches, fewer steps, same tokens). They also demonstrate muP-style transfer with Muon via a "telescoping" correction.

**Bottom line on speedup:** a defensible planning number at 7B is **1.1–1.4x data efficiency vs a *well-tuned* AdamW**, with the top of that range requiring correct width-scaling of LR/WD (or Hyperball-style norm control) — not the 2x in vendor marketing.

### Does muP still matter under Muon?

Evidence says yes, in modified form: (1) [arXiv:2512.05620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05620) (Dec 2025) shows muP LR scaling **plus 1/width weight-decay scaling** is what preserves Muon's speedup across scales; (2) Moonlight/K2/GLM-4.5's **update-RMS matching (0.2)** is itself a muP-flavored per-matrix scale (all three production runs kept it); (3) "Weight Decay may matter more than muP for Learning Rate Transfer in Practice" ([arXiv:2510.19093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19093), Oct 2025) argues muP's assumptions hold only early in training and **weight decay stabilizes update dynamics across widths for the rest** — muP alone is insufficient, not unnecessary; (4) Hyperball II shows norm-controlled training transfers LR better than decoupled WD. Practical reading for this project: keep the muP-style update-scale machinery (i.e., the evidence is *against* `muon_disable_mup_scale=true` as a scale-out default), and treat WD scaling as a first-class transfer knob, not a constant.

**Over-training rationale.** "Beyond Chinchilla-Optimal" ([arXiv:2401.00448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00448), 2023/24) formalizes it: with meaningful inference demand, train **smaller-and-longer**; quality keeps improving to thousands of tokens/param. 2026 practice confirms: nobody ships a competitive 7–8B under ~6T tokens; frontier-competitive small models sit at 15–36T. ## 4. Schedules and hygiene: 2026 norms

- **WSD has effectively won for new runs**: SmolLM3 (WSD, 2000-step warmup, linear decay-to-zero over final ~10%; [HF blog](https://github.com/huggingface/blog/blob/main/smollm3.md), Jul 2025); Kimi K2 (WSD, constant 10T then decay; [arXiv:2507.20534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534)). OLMo 3 is the notable holdout still on **cosine (peak 6e-4, 2000-step warmup, ~10% final LR, GBS 4M tokens, AdamW)** ([OLMo 3 paper PDF](https://kyleclo.com/assets/pdf/olmo-3.pdf), Dec 2025). WSD's practical advantage: extend the stable phase or fork anneals without committing to total tokens up front.
- **Decay-to-zero**: Cerebras' ICLR 2025 paper ([arXiv:2502.15938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15938), Feb 2025) shows linear D2Z beats 10x-decay schedules, with benefits growing with dataset size (their 610M/80TPP D2Z matched 200TPP with 10x-decay — ~60% compute saving).
- **Batch size**: 3–13B norms are **2.4–6M tokens global** (SmolLM3 2.36M; OLMo 3 7B 4M; Nemotron-Nano-2 6.03M). K2's 67M is a 1T-scale outlier. Muon's large-batch tolerance ([arXiv:2505.02222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02222); GLM-4.5 report) justifies ramping to 8–16M mid-run on big clusters; Llama-3-style staged ramps remain standard where used.
- **Stability kit (OLMo 2/3 lineage)**: **QK-norm** (RMSNorm on q,k) and **z-loss** on final logits are both in OLMo 3 ([emergentmind summary of OLMo 3](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/olmo-3), 2025/26); Gemma 3 also uses QK-norm ([arXiv:2503.19786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19786)). **QK-Clip (tau=100)** is the Muon-specific belt-and-suspenders from K2 — it activates rarely and self-deactivates. Weight decay 0.1, grad-clip 1.0, warmup 500–2000 steps are the boring consensus (SmolLM3, K2, OLMo).
- **Embedding handling**: Muon runs keep embeddings/unembeddings (+ norms, biases) on AdamW (Moonlight, GLM-4.5, K2). Embedding-layer LR is a known transfer-sensitive knob ([arXiv:2605.21486](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21486v1), May 2026).
- **Packing hygiene**: intra-document masking (no cross-document attention) is now standard — SmolLM3 and OLMo 3 both use it.

## 5. Multi-stage curricula with public recipes

- **OLMo 3 7B/32B** ([Ai2 blog](https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3), Nov 2025; [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961)): Stage 1 **Dolma 3 Mix ~5.9T** web-heavy @ 8k context; Stage 2 **Dolmino mid-train 100B** (sampled from a 2.2T pool: math, code, QA, instruction, science PDFs); Stage 3 **Longmino 50B** (7B model; 100B for 32B) with **34% long documents / 66% short high-quality**, extending to **65K**.
- **SmolLM3-3B** ([HF blog](https://github.com/huggingface/blog/blob/main/smollm3.md), Jul 2025): Stage 1 0->8T (85% web/12% code/3% math), Stage 2 8->10T (75/15/10), Stage 3 decay 10->11.1T (63/24/13, upsampled high-quality code+math); then long-context mid-train 100B.
- **Qwen3** ([arXiv:2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388), May 2025): S1 >30T general @4k; S2 ~5T knowledge-intensive (STEM/code/reasoning, synthetic from Qwen2.5-Math/Coder); S3 long-context, hundreds of B @32k.
- **Motif-2-12.7B** ([arXiv:2511.07464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07464), Nov 2025): continuous *linear* curriculum from general English toward STEM/math/code over 5.5T — evidence a smooth ramp also works.
- **Granite 4.1** ([IBM Research](https://research.ibm.com/blog/granite-4-1-ai-foundation-models), 2026): 5 phases — broad pretrain (1–2), progressively annealed higher-quality/technical (3–4), long-context to 512K (5).
- The consistent pattern: **web-heavy bulk -> 100B–5T "Dolmino-style" high-quality mid-train (code/math/instruction-adjacent) -> 50–300B long-context -> LR anneal coinciding with the best data**. For an *agentic* target, the mid-train/anneal stages are where tool-use, function-calling, and code data should be concentrated (Qwen3's S2 and OLMo 3's Dolmino are the templates).

## 6. Long-context: the standard 2026 path to 128k at 7B

Composite of Qwen3 / OLMo 3 / SmolLM3 / K2 / Gemma 3:
1. **Pretrain short** (4k–8k) for the bulk of tokens.
2. **ABF/theta raise + mid-train at 32k–64k**: Qwen3 raises RoPE base 10k -> **1M (ABF)** and trains "hundreds of billions" of tokens @32k with a length-stratified mix (75% docs 16–32k, 25% 4–16k) ([arXiv:2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388)); SmolLM3 does 50B @32k (theta 1.5M) + 50B @64k (theta 5M); OLMo 3 does 50B (34% long docs); K2 does 60B @32k after 400B @4k anneal; Gemma 3 pretrains 32k and rescales to 128k at end of pretraining ([arXiv:2503.19786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19786)). **Typical cost: 50–100B tokens for a 3–8B model — ~1–2% of the total budget.**
3. **YaRN for the final 2–4x**: Qwen3-8B 32k -> 131,072 via YaRN(+DCA) at inference; SmolLM3 64k -> 128k via YaRN; K2 -> 128k via YaRN. Cerebras claims a 99%-fewer-tokens variant of extension ([blog](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/extending-llm-context-with-99-less-training-tokens), 2025) if budget is tight.
4. **Attention pattern**: hybrid SWA is the 2026 default — OLMo 3: **SWA 4096 in 3 of 4 layers, full attention every 4th/final layer, YaRN applied only to full-attention layers**; Gemma 3/4: 5:1 local:global (SWA 1024) with only global layers at theta 1M, Gemma 4 interleaves SWA 512–1024 with global layers up to 256K context ([model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4), Jul 2026). Layer-selective YaRN beats uniform theta bumps (Mellum2, [arXiv:2605.31268](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31268), 2026). Document packing + intra-document masking throughout.

## 7. Synthesis for a 7B agentic from-scratch run

The full production stack this project wants already exists in public form: **MuonClip (Muon + WD 0.1 + update-RMS 0.2 + QK-Clip tau=100) + WSD decay-to-zero + QK-norm + z-loss + 3-stage curriculum (web -> Dolmino-style -> long-context) + SWA-hybrid attention + ABF/YaRN to 128k**, each component validated in at least one >=5T-token production run between Feb 2025 and Jun 2026 (Moonlight, K2, GLM-4.5, Motif-2, OLMo 3, Qwen3, SmolLM3). The one place where independent evidence disagrees with vendor claims is the size of Muon's win (1.1–1.4x, not 2x) and its dependence on correct width-scaling of LR/WD — which is precisely the muP machinery this project already validated at 250M->1B.

## OPEN QUESTIONS
- Gemma 4 (released 2026-04-02) does not disclose per-size pretraining token budgets in its model card — the strongest recent Google data point for 7B-class budgets is therefore still Gemma 3 (12T @ 12B, 14T @ 27B).
- No public head-to-head Muon vs well-tuned AdamW exists at exactly dense 7B and >=1T tokens; the independent benchmarks stop at 1.4B params and the production runs (Moonlight/K2/GLM/Motif-2) have no AdamW control arm.
- Stanford-I found SOAP/Kron overtake Muon at >=8-16x Chinchilla overtraining, but this was measured at <=1.2B params — whether it holds at 7B @ ~40x Chinchilla (6T tokens) is untested; a small-scale pilot at the project's actual token/param ratio would resolve it.
- OLMo 3's full hyperparameter table (Table 35, Appendix A.2: exact z-loss coefficient, AdamW betas/eps, grad-clip) could not be extracted from the PDF via web tooling — worth pulling from the OLMo-core GitHub configs (github.com/allenai/OLMo-core, src/scripts/official/OLMo3) before finalizing the config.
- Qwen3's 36T figure is reported family-wide; per-size accounting for Qwen3-8B specifically (and whether smaller sizes saw all 36T) is not broken out in the technical report.
- Whether Hyperball (Jun 2026) is production-ready at 7B is unknown — its 20-30% gains are demonstrated only up to 1.2B and no large production run has adopted it yet; it is the highest-upside/highest-risk optimizer upgrade on the table.
- Batch-size ramp schedules for K2 and GLM-4.5 are not fully public (K2 reports a flat 67M-token batch); the evidence for ramping at 7B is inferential (Muon large-batch tolerance + Llama 3 practice) rather than a published 7B ramp recipe.
- JAX-native distributed Muon parity: Moonshot's open-source distributed Muon and Motif's Parallel Muon kernels are PyTorch; the project's own B200-validated JAX/Keras3 implementation should be benchmarked against their reported communication/memory overheads (UNVERIFIED whether optax-contrib Muon matches Moonshot's ZeRO-1-style distributed variant).
