# Reference architectures for a 7-9B agentic model (2025-2026)

## RECOMMENDATION
Build a dense ~7-8B decoder using OLMo 3 7B as the recipe template (it is the only fully replayable open recipe at exactly this scale, and its Instruct/Think post-training pipeline is the closest open blueprint for the agentic + thinking-mode goals), but adopt the 2026 consensus geometry where OLMo lagged: GQA with 8 KV heads and head_dim 128, QK-norm, SwiGLU, pre+post RMSNorm, and Gemma-4-style interleaved sliding-window attention at 5:1 (1024-token window, local RoPE theta 10k, global theta 1M or p-RoPE 0.25), targeting 32K pretrain context extended to 128-256K via a Longmino-style midtrain + YaRN. Keep your 131k SPM-Unigram vocab, muP, and Muon hybrid — Kimi K2's MuonClip run (15.5T tokens, zero spikes) is the at-scale validation of exactly the optimizer path you have already locked, and your MuonClip safety net is the right hedge. Plan a token budget of at least 10-15T with an agentic/code/reasoning-heavy midtraining stage: OLMo 3's main deficit vs Qwen3 is its 6T budget, not its architecture. Add reserved control tokens for tool-calling and <think> spans from day one, and optionally a detachable Gemma-4-style MTP drafter head (it does not perturb the backbone and buys speculative decoding later).

Do NOT put MoE, MLA, or Gated-DeltaNet/Mamba hybrids on the critical path. Small-MoE is clearly where serving economics are going (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Gemma 4 26B-A4B, Nemotron 3 Nano), but from-scratch MoE adds routing, expert-parallel, and stability risk a solo team should not absorb, and dense-then-upcycle remains a proven later option. MLA never achieved adoption beyond Moonshot and DeepSeek itself has moved past it. The DeltaNet 3:1 hybrid is the most credible research bet (Qwen3.5/3.6 at every size, Olmo-Hybrid-7B claiming 2x data efficiency with an open recipe) — but MiniMax's M2 post-mortem on reasoning deficits at scale plus the thin JAX kernel ecosystem make it a side-branch ablation at 250M-1B scale using your muP ladder, not the main run. If the ablation reproduces Ai2's data-efficiency gains in JAX on B200, revisit for the next model, not this one.

### Gemma 3 (Google, Mar 2025) — the baseline the owner knows
1B/4B/12B/27B dense, 128K context, 5:1 local:global sliding-window attention (1024-token window), RoPE theta 1M on global / 10k on local layers, QK-norm replacing logit softcapping, 262k SentencePiece vocab, Gemma license (not Apache). ### Gemma 4 (Google DeepMind, released 2026-04-02) — owner's explicit ask
- **Sizes:** E2B (~2.3B effective / 5B total, per-layer embeddings), E4B (~4.5B effective / 8B total), **12B dense** ("unified" encoder-free multimodal, released June 2026), **26B-A4B MoE (26B total / 3.8B active)**, **31B dense**. Sources: Gemma 4 Technical Report, https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02770v1 (July 2026); https://developers.googleblog.com/bring-state-of-the-art-agentic-skills-to-the-edge-with-gemma-4/ (2026-04-02).

- **Attention:** interleaved local SWA : global at **4:1 (E2B) / 5:1 (all others), final layer always global**; sliding windows of **512 (small dense) / 1024 (larger)** tokens; **QK-norm; pre-norm AND post-norm RMSNorm (sandwich)**; global layers use **p-RoPE with p=0.25**, RoPE frequency **1M global / 10k local**; **keys re-used as values** in global layers (except E2B/E4B) cutting global KV cache ~37.5%; KV-cache cross-layer sharing on E2B/E4B (ratios 20/35 and 18/42). Source: tech report + https://developers.googleblog.com/gemma-4-12b-the-developer-guide/ (June 2026).

## 3. Trend assessment

**Dense vs MoE at this scale for agentic use.** Every major lab's small *agentic serving* flagship in 2026 is a small-MoE (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Gemma 4 26B-A4B, Nemotron 3 Nano, gpt-oss-20b, Granite H-Tiny). MoE wins tokens/sec/$ at serving time. But for a **solo from-scratch team** the calculus differs: MoE adds router/load-balancing losses, expert-parallel sharding, upcycling/init subtleties, 3–5x checkpoint memory for the same active compute, and much harder debugging — while a dense 7–9B fits trivially on one B200 in BF16 and keeps the muP + Muon story clean (muP transfer and Muon are both dense-validated in this project). Dense-then-upcycle is a proven de-risked path (Qwen and NVIDIA both upcycled dense checkpoints into MoEs).

**Hybrid SSM / linear attention maturity.** The 2026 momentum is real: Qwen shipped Gated DeltaNet hybrids at *every* size including 9B dense; Ai2's Olmo-Hybrid-7B claims 2x data efficiency with a fully open recipe; Kimi-Linear, Nemotron 3, Granite 4, Falcon-H1 all ship hybrids. The strongest counter-evidence is MiniMax's public post-mortem: **M2 reverted to full attention** because no efficient-attention variant reliably matched full attention on reasoning/coding/agent tasks in production, deficits appeared only at scale in multi-hop reasoning, and the kernel/infra ecosystem is immature (https://www.minimax.io/news/why-did-m2-end-up-as-a-full-attention-model, Nov 2025; https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2025-11-04-miminmax-m2/). For a JAX/Keras3 solo team, DeltaNet-class kernels (flash-linear-attention) are Triton/PyTorch-first; JAX production kernels are thin — that pushes hybrids into research-risk territory *for this stack*, despite being clearly "the direction."

**The proven middle path is interleaved sliding-window attention.** Gemma 3/4 (5:1, 512–1024 windows), OLMo 3 (3:1, 4K window), gpt-oss (1:1, 128 window), MiMo-V2-Flash (5:1, 128 window) all converge here. SWA interleaving is supported by every inference stack (vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp, TRT-LLM), costs nothing in quality at these ratios, and slashes KV cache and long-context compute.

**MLA adoption outside DeepSeek is narrow** — essentially Moonshot (K2; Kimi-Linear's global layers). GLM chose GQA + partial RoPE; Qwen chose GQA/hybrid; and DeepSeek itself moved past MLA in V4. GQA (4–8 KV heads) + QK-norm is the 2026 default for ≤15B. Skip MLA.

**Universal 2026 consensus items (proven-safe):** decoder-only, RMSNorm pre-norm (+ post-norm/sandwich in Gemma 4 and OLMo 3), **QK-norm (now in Qwen3, OLMo 3, Gemma 3/4, GLM-4.5)**, GQA, SwiGLU, RoPE with dual theta (local 10k / global 1M or p-RoPE), no logit softcapping (Gemma dropped it for QK-norm in Gemma 3), untied embeddings at ≥7B, large vocab (131k–262k), native tool-calling control tokens, hybrid thinking/non-thinking mode with `<think>` spans, train-short-extend-long (8–32K pretrain → YaRN/midtrain to 128–256K). **Moderate-risk but trending:** MTP head (Qwen3.6, MiMo-V2, Gemma 4 drafter) — best implemented Gemma-style as a detachable drafter head. **Research-risk for this team:** Gated DeltaNet/Mamba hybrids in JAX, MLA, NoPE-only, native-1M context, from-scratch MoE.

## 4. Best template and deviations

**Template: OLMo 3 7B, with Gemma 4's attention geometry and Qwen3-8B's GQA grafted on.** OLMo 3 is the only model at exactly the target scale whose *entire* recipe — 6T-token Dolma 3 data mix, staged midtraining/long-context curricula, hyperparameters, checkpoints, SFT→DPO→GRPO agentic post-training, async RL code — is open and replayable, which is worth more to a solo team than a few benchmark points. Its architecture is already 90% of the 2026 consensus (SWA 3:1, QK-norm, post-norm, SwiGLU). Deviations worth taking: (1) **GQA 8 KV heads** instead of the 7B's MHA (as OLMo's own 32B and Qwen3-8B do); (2) **Gemma-4-style 5:1 local:global with 1024-token window, local theta 10k / global theta 1M (or p-RoPE 0.25), targeting 128–256K context** instead of OLMo's 65K ceiling; (3) **bigger token budget** — OLMo's 6T is the known weakness vs Qwen's 36T; target ≥10–15T with an agentic/code-heavy midtrain; (4) keep the project's 131k SPM-Unigram vocab, muP, and Muon — Muon-class training is now validated at 15.5T tokens/1T params via K2's MuonClip (the project's existing MuonClip safety net is exactly the right hedge); (5) optional **detachable MTP drafter head** (Gemma 4 style) for speculative decoding; (6) run **one small ablation branch of a 3:1 Gated DeltaNet hybrid** (Olmo-Hybrid-7B as the open reference) only if JAX kernels prove out — do not put it on the critical path.
