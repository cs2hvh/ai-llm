## FULL REPORT
# Does Muon at 7B require muP-style update scaling? Reconciling `muon_disable_mup_scale=true` with the Dec-2025 transfer paper and the Kimi K2 recipe

**Research date: 2026-07-23. All sources fetched live. Note: this session's WebSearch budget was exhausted by prior work, so discovery was done via the arXiv API + direct fetches of primary sources; all load-bearing claims below are grounded in live-fetched primary documents.**

## Executive resolution

The apparent contradiction dissolves once one fact is made precise: **"muP scaling" for Muon is NOT the AdamW muP rule (LR × 1/width) — it is the spectral rule (per-layer LR × sqrt(d_out/d_in)), which is width-INDEPENDENT for a fixed-aspect-ratio transformer family.** The project's `muon_disable_mup_scale=true` flag disables an AdamW-style 1/width_mult multiplier that was being stacked *on top of* optax's Muon — and optax's Muon already applies the correct spectral aspect-ratio factor internally. Disabling the 1/width multiplier therefore *implements* the muP-for-Muon rule from the Dec-2025 paper rather than violating it. The committed decision is correct in substance — but the config's rationale and the optimizer-dimension's warning were both operating on a category confusion, and one genuinely dangerous incompatibility remains: **Kimi-K2-style update-RMS matching (0.2·sqrt(max(m,n))) is mutually exclusive with muP width transfer of the learning rate.** Adopting "the K2 configuration wholesale" while keeping a muP-transferred LR would combine two conventions that each work alone but are incorrect together.

## (1) The Dec-2025 paper: exact claim and conditions

The paper is **"Hyperparameter Transfer Enables Consistent Gains of Matrix-Preconditioned Optimizers Across Scales"**, Qiu, Chen, Phan, Lei, Wilson — arXiv:2512.05620, submitted 2025-12-05, revised 2026-01-18 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05620, fetched 2026-07-23).

Exact claims, from the full text (https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05620v2, fetched 2026-07-23):

- **Optimizers**: Shampoo, SOAP, Muon vs tuned AdamW, on Llama-architecture models **190M → 1.4B params**, FineWeb, GPT-2 tokenizer, **20 tokens/param** (largest run ≈ 27B tokens).
- **LR rule for Muon**: per their Table 1, Muon's per-layer LR should scale as **"√(d_out/d_in)"** — the spectral/muP rule for Muon. This is *not* 1/width; for square hidden matrices and fixed FFN expansion ratio it is **constant in width**.
- **Weight decay**: "scaling the independent weight decay as 1/width is nearly optimal across optimizers" — i.e., decoupled-from-LR weight decay ∝ 1/D, which is "distinct from the standard μP prescription of Θ(1) weight decay."
- **Consequence of wrong scaling**: "Muon, SOAP and Shampoo consistently achieve near 1.4× speedup over AdamW" when properly scaled, whereas "the speedup vanishes rapidly with scale under incorrect scaling" — Figure 6 shows standard parametrization (SP) collapsing from ~1.4× at 190M to ~1.0× at 1.4B. Note the failure mode is **silent efficiency loss, not divergence**.
- **Directly relevant to the K2 recipe**: the paper explicitly rejects RMS matching for width transfer: "several recent large-scale investigations of Muon have resorted to heuristics for scaling its learning rate with width, such as … matching the update RMS of Adam [30], both are incorrect as we show in Appendix G."
- Finite-width deviations from μP can be mitigated by **blocking (block size 512)** and **explicit spectral normalization** ("normalizes the update spectral norm to exactly (rather than asymptotically) √(d_out/d_in)").

**Scale caveat**: everything was measured at ≤1.4B params and 20 TPP (~27B tokens). A 7B/6T-token run is ~5× wider-model and ~40× longer-horizon than anything in this paper; its weight-decay finding in particular is an extrapolation.

Supporting 2026 work: "Extending μP: Spectral Conditions for Feature Learning Across Optimizers" (arXiv:2602.20937, 2026-02-24, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20937) derives spectral-condition μP rules for Muon among others and "demonstrate[s] zero-shot learning rate transfer across increasing model width"; "Towards a Principled Muon under μP" (arXiv:2601.01306, 2026-01-04) notes existing Muon-under-μP setups "do not ensure that the spectral conditions hold throughout the entire training horizon" and argues "maintaining spectral control at the level of optimizer updates alone is sufficient to preserve μP-compatible scaling." (Both fetched 2026-07-23; scale details of these two not extracted — secondary support only.)

## (2) What the production runs actually did — none used muP width scaling

**Moonlight (Moonshot, 3B/16B-A3B MoE, 5.7T tokens)** — "Muon is Scalable for LLM Training", arXiv:2502.16982, 2025-02-24 (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16982v1, fetched 2026-07-23). Two modifications: (a) weight decay — "both the weight and the layer output's RMS keep growing … exceeding the high-precision range of bf16 … we introduced the standard AdamW weight decay mechanism" (vanilla Muon without WD degrades/diverges at scale, their Figure 2); (b) update-RMS matching — update scaled by **0.2 · sqrt(max(A,B))** because "AdamW's update RMS is usually around 0.2 to 0.4", validated in their Table 8 ("0.2 RMS and 0.4 RMS performed similarly and much better than other settings"). Purpose is explicit: "with this adjustment, Muon can directly reuse the learning rate and weight decay tuned for AdamW." **Zero mentions of muP** anywhere in the paper. Hyperparameters: peak LR 4.2e-4 → 4.2e-5, WD 0.1 all stages. Their "no hyper-parameter tuning" claim means *reuse of AdamW-tuned HPs at the same architecture/scale*, not width transfer.

**Kimi K2 (1.04T total / 32.6B active MoE, 15.5T tokens)** — arXiv:2507.20534 (https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20534v1, fetched 2026-07-23). MuonClip = Muon + weight decay + "consistent update RMS scaling" + QK-Clip. Algorithm 1 line 5: "O_t = Newton-Schulz(M_t) · √max(n,m) · 0.2" annotated "Match Adam RMS". QK-Clip "rescal[es] the query and key projection weights post-update to bound the growth of attention logits", **τ=100**, and "the maximum logits gradually decay to a typical operating range without requiring any adjustment to τ." Pretraining: 500-step warmup, **constant LR 2e-4 for the first 10T tokens, cosine decay 2e-4→2e-5 over the last 5.5T**, WD 0.1 throughout, batch 67M tokens. **No muP, no width-scaled LR**; architecture/HP choices came from "carefully controlled small-scale experiments" and a Muon-specific MoE sparsity scaling law — i.e., a scaling-law ladder, not muP zero-shot transfer.

**GLM-4.5 (355B total / 32B active, 23T tokens)** — arXiv:2508.06471, 2025-08-08 (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2508.06471, fetched 2026-07-23). "We employed the Muon optimizer for all parameters except word embedding, bias, and weights for RMSNorm … scaled Muon's update RMS to 0.2." Warmup to **2.5e-4**, cosine decay to 2.5e-5; WD 0.1; batch ramped 16M→64M tokens; **QK-Norm** ("to stabilize the range of attention logits") rather than QK-Clip. **No muP / width-scaled LR discussion; no z-loss found** in the fetched text (the "z-loss" element of the planned recipe could not be verified from GLM-4.5 — flag as UNVERIFIED-in-this-source).

**Motif-2-12.7B (dense, 5.5T tokens, FP8)** — arXiv:2511.07464, 2025-11-07 (https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07464v1, fetched 2026-07-23). Uses **MuonClip** (which by construction embeds K2's 0.2·sqrt(max(n,m)) RMS matching) with WSD schedule, peak LR **4e-4**, batch ramp 16M→80M tokens; hidden size 4096, 40 layers, Grouped Differential Attention. **Zero references to muP or width-scaled LR.** No divergences or loss spikes reported. Two caveats: the report does not state its QK-Clip τ (UNVERIFIED), and the 12.7B was initialized from the smaller Motif model via a "hyperclon[ing]" procedure "strictly preserving the original parameter topology" — so it is not a fully from-scratch data point (the fetched text supports the hypercloning quote; the exact source model was not extracted — partially UNVERIFIED).

**Summary table of the production evidence**: every large Muon run (16B → 1T params, 5.5T → 23T tokens) used **update-RMS matching + one global LR (2e-4–4.2e-4) + flat WD 0.1 + a logit-stability guard (QK-Clip or QK-Norm)**, and **none** used muP width scaling. But none of them *relied on width transfer of the LR either* — they tuned at or near target scale. This is the crux: the K2 recipe and muP transfer are alternative HP-selection strategies, not composable layers.

## (3) Provenance of "Muon needs no muP scaling" — and is there any 7B+ failure report?

The claim has three legitimate roots:

1. **Keller Jordan's original Muon writeup** (https://kellerjordan.github.io/posts/muon/, fetched 2026-07-23) — defines Muon for "the hidden weights"; "AdamW should be used for the embedding and final classifier head layers." It makes **no claim about LR transfer across width** at all, and explicitly lists "Will Muon scale to larger trainings? (e.g., 20B+ parameters for 1T+ tokens)" as an open question. So citing Keller for "no muP needed" was always an over-read — matching the project's own 2026-05-18 post-review finding that primary sources were silent.
2. **The norm-constrained/LMO line** — Scion, "Training Deep Learning Models with Norm-Constrained LMOs" (arXiv:2502.07529, 2025-02-11, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07529): norm-constrained updates "lead… to the transferability of hyperparameters across model sizes" *without reparametrization*, tested at nanoGPT scale. Bernstein & Newhouse's dual-norm perspective (arXiv:2409.20325, referenced as the basis of optax's `adaptive` option) is the theoretical backbone. This is real support — but it supports *spectral aspect-ratio scaling inside the update*, which orthodox Muon implementations already perform; it does not support stacking 1/width on top, nor RMS matching.
3. **Moonlight's "out-of-the-box" language** (above) — which is about AdamW-HP reuse at fixed scale, not width transfer.

**Counter-evidence at scale**: "Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers and Where to Find Them" (Wen, Hall, Ma, Liang, arXiv:2509.02046, 2025-09-02, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046) tuned every optimizer per scale (0.1B–1.2B, 1–8× Chinchilla) and found Muon-class speedups shrink "1.4x over AdamW for 0.1B parameter models to merely 1.1x for 1.2B" — while 2512.05620 claims the 1.4× is *retained* to 1.4B under correct transfer. These two respectable studies are in unresolved tension; treat "Muon gives ≥1.3× at 7B" as optimistic, not guaranteed.

**Divergence from disabling muP at 7B+**: no primary source reports divergence attributable to absent muP width scaling — the documented Muon failure modes at scale are (a) missing weight decay (Moonlight Fig. 2 — weights/activations exceed bf16 range) and (b) attention-logit explosions (K2's motivation for QK-Clip; K2 reports MuonClip produced "zero loss spikes" over 15.5T tokens). The realistic penalty for a wrong scaling convention is the one 2512.05620 measures: **silently losing the speedup you bought Muon for**, plus landing off the LR optimum at 7B if you transferred an LR under a convention that doesn't transfer.

## (4) What the committed flag actually does — code-level resolution

From `D:\work\ai-llm\src\myllm\training\optimizer.py` (lines 231–267) and `D:\work\ai-llm\configs\base_1b.yaml` (lines 108–136): the hidden bucket runs `optax.contrib.muon` and then, unless `muon_disable_mup_scale=true`, chains `optax.scale(1.0 / mup_width_mult)` — an **AdamW-muP hidden-bucket rule (1/8 at 1B; would be ~1/16 at 7B)** applied to Muon's already-orthonormalized update. Meanwhile, the optax source (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/optax/main/optax/contrib/_muon.py, fetched 2026-07-23) shows the default path already applies `scale = jnp.sqrt(jnp.maximum(1, fan_out / fan_in))` — which the optax docstring itself frames as the width-transfer scaling — and offers `consistent_rms` ("0.2 is recommended to match AdamW's empirical RMS") as the *alternative*, K2-style convention.

So with the flag **true**, the effective hidden-matrix rule is LR × sqrt(max(1, fan_out/fan_in)) ≈ the spectral μP-for-Muon rule from 2512.05620 Table 1 (√(d_out/d_in)) — width-independent, transferable. With the flag **false**, Muon's hidden LR is divided by width_mult *twice over the correct convention*, which is precisely why the 2026-05-20 GPU smoke saw Muon descend at only 63% of AdamW's rate with the scale on and reach parity with it off. The smoke didn't validate "Muon needs no muP"; it detected a genuine double-scaling bug. (Minor deviation to note: optax floors the factor at 1, so a 4d→d down-projection gets scale 1 instead of the strict spectral 0.5 — width-independent either way, but worth a unit test documenting the convention.)

## (5) Pre-run transfer check — what to measure before spending ~$500k

1. **Width-transfer LR sweep under the final convention** (flag=true, optax default scaling, no `consistent_rms`): train ≥3 widths on the existing base_width=256 chain (e.g., 256/512/1024/2048-hidden) plus one larger rung near 7B shape, ≥20 tokens/param each, with the **full WSD decay executed** (optima move after decay). Pass criterion: optimal peak LR flat across widths (within half a 2× grid step) and loss curves collapse after muP alignment. This mirrors Essential AI's telescoping methodology ("Practical Efficiency of Muon for Pretraining", arXiv:2505.02222, https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02222v4, fetched 2026-07-23), which demonstrated μP-calibrated Muon to 3.7B/160B tokens with "variation … at the two largest scales … not statistically significant".
2. **Weight-decay arm**: 0.1 flat (industry convention, coupled λ·η as optax implements — "this weight decay is multiplied with the learning rate") vs an independent-WD arm scaled 1/width per 2512.05620. Judge on *post-decay* val loss, not mid-run loss.
3. **Negative control**: repeat one width pair with `consistent_rms=0.2` and confirm the optimal LR *shifts* with width (expected ∝1/sqrt(width) drift) — this empirically demonstrates why K2-style RMS matching must not be mixed with a transferred LR (2512.05620 Appendix G).
4. **Health telemetry to log at every rung and during the 7B run**: per-layer update spectral norm and RMS (should follow the chosen convention), weight-norm growth (Moonlight's bf16-range failure mode), and max attention logits (K2's QK-Clip trigger stat; enable `muonclip_threshold=100.0` for the 7B run — K2's τ=100 was benign at 15.5T tokens).
5. **Do not treat the 500-step smoke as evidence**: WD equilibrium effects, logit growth, and finite-width μP deviations all manifest at 10^3–10^5× that horizon.
