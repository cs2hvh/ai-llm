# FOLLOWUP: End-to-end stack decision: stay on JAX/Keras3 vs migrate to PyTorch/torchtitan (and the train-JAX / serve-and-RL-PyTorch bridge)

This is the single unresolved contradiction that gates almost every downstream engineering choice, yet the eight dimensions silently assume different answers. The frameworks dimension says abandon the existing ~39K-LOC JAX/Keras3 codebase for PyTorch/torchtitan (all at-scale Blackwell pretraining evidence is PyTorch; zero precedent for a 7B Keras3 run). The optimizer and cluster-economics dimensions instead assume the run STAYS on JAX/Keras3 (GSPMD data-axis sharding, benchmarking optax-contrib Muon). The agentic post-training dimension says every mature agentic-RL framework (verl, SkyRL, PRIME-RL, NeMo-RL, slime) is PyTorch and proposes a weight-conversion bridge. Nobody costed the migration end-to-end or proved the hybrid path works. Choosing wrong, or leaving this unresolved, risks a mid-project rewrite of the pretraining core, a stranded Muon implementation, an unusable checkpoint format, and a broken serving path — with ~$500k of compute downstream. A seasoned lead resolves this FIRST because it determines the optimizer port, checkpoint interop, the RL stack, and the serving stack simultaneously.

## RECOMMENDATION
Choose the hybrid path (c), and resolve the intra-JAX question with a measured gate rather than a rewrite: keep pretraining in JAX (where your Muon-on-B200 validation, muP 250M→1B transfer, and 39K LOC of tested code are live assets, and where NVIDIA ships supported Blackwell containers with CI on B200/B300), and commit now to PyTorch for everything after the base model — weight conversion to HF safetensors, agentic RL with verl (multi-turn tool-calling, FSDP2 + vLLM/SGLang rollout), and vLLM/SGLang serving. This is the pattern proven at 8B–12.7T-token scale (Marin 8B) and industrially by Gemma; a full torchtitan migration is the only option that strands your validated distributed Muon (torchtitan has NO merged distributed Muon as of 2026-07-23 — issue #2494 was closed as duplicate and all distributed-Muon PRs are open drafts), forces muP re-validation, and burns an UNVERIFIED-but-plausible 6–12 solo person-weeks plus repeat pilot compute before the first of 6T tokens flows.

Three conditions make this safe, all front-loaded and cheap relative to the ~$140k swing that 30% vs 40% MFU implies on your ~$500k budget: (1) run a 1-week single-node B200 bake-off of your Keras3/JAX stack on a Llama-3-8B-shaped config against torchtitan's published reference (5,762–8,532 TPS/GPU on H100) — if your stack cannot reach competitive throughput or scale past one node, fall back to MaxText (stays in JAX, keeps Muon/MuonClip natively, NVIDIA-supported on Blackwell) rather than torchtitan, since Keras3-at-7B is the unprecedented element, not JAX; (2) keep the architecture Llama/Qwen-shaped wherever the agentic goals allow, and fold muP multipliers into exported weights, so vLLM's Transformers backend serves it day-one; (3) build the Orbax→safetensors exporter plus a per-position logit-parity harness (fp32 and serving dtype) BEFORE the big run and CI-test it on the existing 1B checkpoint — the Gemma PR #29402 failure modes (activation variant, norm upcast, scale rounding) are exactly what it must catch. Do not attempt agentic RL in JAX on GPUs: Tunix is TPU-only in practice, and every mature GPU agentic-RL stack is PyTorch.

## Q1. JAX on Blackwell: published performance and non-NVIDIA precedent

**What exists (all vendor-sourced):**
- NVIDIA's JAX-Toolbox is actively maintained for Blackwell: NGC JAX containers 26.04–26.06 (CUDA 13.3), with MaxText, AXLearn, Equinox supported; public CI runs on H100, and NVIDIA states internal CI runs on **B200, GB200, B300 SXM6, GB300** (https://github.com/NVIDIA/JAX-Toolbox, fetched 2026-07-23).
- NVIDIA blog "Train Models Faster with JAX and MaxText Using NVFP4 on NVIDIA Blackwell" (2026-06-08, NVIDIA-authored): **Llama 3 8B trained with JAX/MaxText on GB200 (4 GPUs) at 1,497–2,301 TFLOP/s per GPU**, NVFP4 giving 1.35× over FP8 on GB200 and 1.31× on GB300; Llama 3.1 405B on 128 GPUs at 1,557–3,633 TF/s, 1.44–1.73× over FP8, with "+0.026 nats" mean loss gap after convergence (https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/train-models-faster-with-jax-and-maxtext-using-nvfp4-on-nvidia-blackwell/). **No absolute MFU% is published**; TF/s at 4-bit precision is not directly comparable to bf16 MFU.
- MaxText's own README/changelog advertises "high MFU" but publishes no B200 dense-8B MFU table; it added **Muon support 2025-12-22, MuonClip 2026-04-13, RL for Qwen3-30B/GPT-OSS-20B 2026-07-15** (https://github.com/AI-Hypercomputer/maxtext, fetched 2026-07-23).

**What does not exist:** I found **zero non-NVIDIA-authored, at-scale, public JAX-on-Blackwell pretraining runs**. The known independent JAX pretrains (Marin 8B — 12.7T tokens via Levanter; OpenLLaMA 3B/7B/13B via EasyLM) were all on **TPU** (https://huggingface.co/marin-community/marin-8b-base; https://github.com/openlm-research/open_llama). Levanter itself says "GPU support is still in-progress" (https://github.com/stanford-crfm/levanter, fetched 2026-07-23). Absence of evidence after direct checks of MaxText, JAX-Toolbox, and Levanter — treat "JAX works well on B200" as *vendor-supported but community-unproven*.

**PyTorch counter-evidence (also partly vendor/partner):** torchtitan+torchao ran **MXFP8 Llama3-70B on up to 1,856 B200s** (Crusoe cluster) at 1.22–1.28× over bf16 with matching loss curves (PyTorch blog, 2025-09-03, https://pytorch.org/blog/accelerating-2k-scale-pre-training-up-to-1-28x-with-torchao-mxfp8-and-torchtitan-on-crusoe-b200-cluster/). torchtitan's published dense-8B numbers are H100: **Llama 3.1 8B, 8×H100: 5,762 TPS/GPU bf16 FSDP; 8,532 TPS/GPU with compile+Float8; 33–39% MFU without Float8; 8,380 TPS/GPU at 128 GPUs** (https://github.com/pytorch/torchtitan/blob/main/benchmarks/llama3_h100_202412_torchtitan.md, Dec 2024). torchtitan explicitly supports MXFP8 "on Blackwell GPUs" and Llama 3.1 8B out of the box (https://github.com/pytorch/torchtitan, v0.2.0 Oct 2025).

**Crucial nuance:** the "zero precedent" claim in the frameworks dimension is about **Keras3**, and that part survives scrutiny — there is no public 7B-scale Keras3 pretraining run anywhere, and Keras 3's distribution API is **"only implemented for the JAX backend for now"** (verbatim, https://keras.io/guides/distribution/, fetched 2026-07-23). But *JAX itself* on Blackwell is a first-class NVIDIA-supported target with CI on B200/B300. The precedent gap is Keras3-specific, not JAX-specific.

## Q2. Distributed Muon: torchtitan #2494 and the JAX-vs-PyTorch maturity ledger

**torchtitan #2494 did NOT merge.** It was opened 2026-03-05 requesting Muon+AdamW hybrid with FSDP2 support and was **closed the same day as a duplicate** (of an internal-numbered issue), per the issue timeline (https://github.com/pytorch/torchtitan/issues/2494). As of 2026-07-23, per the GitHub PR search (https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo:pytorch/torchtitan+muon+type:pr):
- **No distributed Muon has merged into torchtitan.** Open/draft PRs: #3971 "SPMD-typed Muon with DTensor" (draft, opened **2026-07-22 — yesterday**), #3719 FlexShard Muon RFC (2026-06-19), #3502 communication-free Muon (draft, 2026-06-03), #1630 Distributed Scion/Muon (open since **2025-08-25**, ~11 months unmerged).
- What *did* merge is plumbing: mixed-optimizer support #3269 (2026-06-04) and param_groups #2859 (2026-04-07).
- `torch.optim.Muon` exists in PyTorch core (2.13 docs) but the docs describe a single-device optimizer for 2D params with **no mention of FSDP/DTensor/sharded support** (https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.13/generated/torch.optim.Muon.html); torchtitan issue #2494 itself states "PyTorch 2.9's torch.optim.Muon does not yet support distributed training."
- Outside torchtitan, PyTorch does have production-grade distributed Muon: **Moonshot's open-source ZeRO-1-style distributed Muon** (Megatron-based), validated on Moonlight 16B-MoE/5.7T tokens with "~52% of the training FLOPs" vs AdamW (https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Moonlight), and **Motif's Parallel Muon + MuonClip** used to train Motif 2 12.7B (arXiv 2511.07464, 2025-11-07). Both are Megatron-世界, not torchtitan.

**JAX side:** `optax.contrib.muon` exists upstream (https://optax.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/contrib.html), and — the strongest datapoint — **MaxText runs Muon (since 2025-12-22) and MuonClip (2026-04-13) under GSPMD sharding in production configs** (https://github.com/AI-Hypercomputer/maxtext). In JAX, Newton–Schulz is just matmuls inside jit; GSPMD inserts collectives automatically, so "distributed Muon" is not a separate engineering artifact the way it is in FSDP2 (this composition argument is reasoning, but MaxText's shipped Muon is the empirical anchor). The team's own Muon hybrid is already **validated on B200 in their JAX code** (project context).

**Net:** for *this team*, the Muon asset is strictly more mature on the JAX side. A torchtitan migration today means either abandoning Muon for AdamW, adopting an unmerged draft PR, or porting Moonshot's Megatron implementation — all real costs.

## Q3. Checkpoint interop JAX/Orbax → HF/PyTorch

- **Mature for Llama-shaped architectures:** Marin 8B was trained entirely in JAX (Levanter) to 12.7T tokens and released directly as HF safetensors, "works with standard Transformers library and vLLM since it uses the standard Llama architecture," Apache 2.0 (https://huggingface.co/marin-community/marin-8b-base, May 2025). Levanter has built-in HF import/export via SafeTensors (https://github.com/stanford-crfm/levanter). OpenLLaMA (JAX/EasyLM, 3B/7B/13B, 1T tokens) shipped dual EasyLM+PyTorch weights (https://github.com/openlm-research/open_llama).
- **Documented failures exist, and they are subtle:** the Gemma JAX→HF port — done by Google+HF, the best-resourced conversion team on earth — shipped with **three numeric-fidelity bugs** fixed only in transformers PR #29402 (merged 2024-03-19): exact-vs-approximate GELU mismatch, layernorm computed in bf16 instead of fp32-upcast, and the embedding scale sqrt(3072)=55.4256 rounding to 55.5 in bf16 (https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/pull/29402). OpenLLaMA additionally documented that "the auto-converted fast tokenizer sometimes gives incorrect tokenizations" (https://github.com/openlm-research/open_llama).
- **Implication for a custom architecture** (custom norms, muP multipliers in modeling code): conversion is a *write-it-yourself* artifact — a custom HF modeling file plus a weight/config exporter, with muP scalars either folded into weights or reproduced in the modeling code, and a logit-parity test harness. No off-the-shelf Orbax→HF tool handles arbitrary custom architectures (UNVERIFIED as a universal negative, but consistent with vLLM/HF docs below). The Gemma episode is the canonical warning: parity must be tested per-position on real prompts, in the serving dtype.

## Q4. Serving: does vLLM/SGLang take a JAX model day-one?

**On NVIDIA GPUs — no.** vLLM's add-a-model docs require a PyTorch model implementation; the escape hatch is that "many decoder language models can now be automatically loaded using the Transformers modeling backend" — i.e., an HF-format checkpoint + HF-compatible (PyTorch) modeling code, with no JAX path mentioned (https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/contributing/model/index.html, fetched 2026-07-23). SGLang's runtime is PyTorch-based and "compatible with most Hugging Face models"; its JAX variant, **SGLang-JAX, runs natively on TPU only** (announced Oct 2025) (https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang, v0.5.15.post1, 2026-07-14). So **serving on B200/B300 requires an HF/PyTorch-format artifact regardless of training framework** — this cost is symmetric across options (a), (b), (c); option (b) merely pays it earlier.

## Q5. Solo migration effort Keras3 → torchtitan

**No documented Keras3→torchtitan (or Keras3→any-PyTorch-trainer) migration write-up exists that I could find — the estimate below is UNVERIFIED reasoning.** Anchors: torchtitan provides trainer, FSDP2/TP/PP/CP, checkpointing, and Llama 3.1 8B configs out of the box (https://github.com/pytorch/torchtitan; arXiv 2410.06511 v3, 2025-06-07). What torchtitan does *not* provide: the team's Muon hybrid distributed (Q2 — not merged), muP plumbing, their data pipeline (13-source multilingual + 131k SPM tokenizer integration), and their eval/governance harness. Realistic solo estimate: model+config port 1–2 wk (if Llama-shaped), data loader 1–2 wk, optimizer 2–4 wk (port Moonshot ZeRO-1 Muon or adopt draft PR #3971 and own it), muP re-verification + 250M pilot re-run + loss-parity debugging 2–4 wk ⇒ **6–12 person-weeks before the first at-scale token**, plus repeat pilot compute. The larger, unquantifiable risk is silently losing the already-validated muP/Muon transfer results and having no A/B baseline in the new stack.

## Q6. Is train-JAX → convert → RL+serve-PyTorch proven at 7B+?

**Yes — it is the standard industry pattern, at far beyond 7B:**
- **Gemma** (all generations): trained in JAX on TPU, released as HF/PyTorch weights, served day-one by vLLM/SGLang; the whole community does PyTorch RL/finetuning on these JAX-trained weights.
- **Marin 8B**: JAX/Levanter, 12.7T tokens → HF safetensors → transformers/vLLM out-of-the-box (https://huggingface.co/marin-community/marin-8b-base).
- **OpenLLaMA 7B/13B**: JAX/EasyLM → dual-format release (https://github.com/openlm-research/open_llama).
- What breaks, per documented cases: activation/precision mismatches (Gemma PR #29402), tokenizer conversion (OpenLLaMA fast-tokenizer warning). All are catchable with a parity harness.

**RL stack:** verl v0.8.0 (2026-06-01) is PyTorch-only — FSDP/FSDP2/Megatron training backends, vLLM≥0.8.2/SGLang rollout, "multi-turn with tool calling," proven to 671B (https://github.com/volcengine/verl). The one JAX counter-example, Google's **Tunix** (v0.1.7, June 2026; agentic multi-turn tool-use RL since Dec 2025, vLLM + SGLang-JAX rollout), is explicitly TPU-focused with **no documented GPU support or roadmap** (https://github.com/google/tunix). On rented NVIDIA clusters, agentic RL is effectively PyTorch-land — the hybrid bridge is mandatory for the RL stage no matter what.
