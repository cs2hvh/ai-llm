## GAP: End-to-end stack decision: stay on JAX/Keras3 vs migrate to PyTorch/torchtitan (and the train-JAX / serve-and-RL-PyTorch bridge)
This is the single unresolved contradiction that gates almost every downstream engineering choice, yet the eight dimensions silently assume different answers. The frameworks dimension says abandon the existing ~39K-LOC JAX/Keras3 codebase for PyTorch/torchtitan (all at-scale Blackwell pretraining evidence is PyTorch; zero precedent for a 7B Keras3 run). The optimizer and cluster-economics dimensions instead assume the run STAYS on JAX/Keras3 (GSPMD data-axis sharding, benchmarking optax-contrib Muon). The agentic post-training dimension says every mature agentic-RL framework (verl, SkyRL, PRIME-RL, NeMo-RL, slime) is PyTorch and proposes a weight-conversion bridge. Nobody costed the migration end-to-end or proved the hybrid path works. Choosing wrong, or leaving this unresolved, risks a mid-project rewrite of the pretraining core, a stranded Muon implementation, an unusable checkpoint format, and a broken serving path — with ~$500k of compute downstream. A seasoned lead resolves this FIRST because it determines the optimizer port, checkpoint interop, the RL stack, and the serving stack simultaneously.

## GAP: Engineering scope, tooling, and cost of the agentic RL environment / verifiable-reward fleet
The agentic post-training plan specifies the RL algorithm (GRPO/RLVR via verl or SkyRL) and correctly notes rollout throughput (vLLM/SGLang) will bottleneck, but NO dimension scoped the environment infrastructure — the sandboxed code/terminal execution, SWE-repo Docker harnesses, tool-execution services, verifiable reward computation, and the orchestration to run hundreds of thousands of concurrent multi-turn rollouts with flaky-environment and reward-hacking handling. For an agentic model this environment fleet IS the substance of the RL stage, not a detail, and it is the usual place small/solo teams stall for months. The current plan budgets agentic RL at 5-15% of pretraining FLOPs and 'weeks not months,' which almost certainly ignores the environment-building and operations effort. Getting this wrong changes the timeline, the cost, and whether the headline agentic targets (SWE-bench Verified 15-25%, Terminal-Bench 2.0, tau2-bench) are reachable at all.

## GAP: Tokenizer decision (three-way contradiction) plus sovereign-language data sufficiency for the multilingual differentiator
Three dimensions give three incompatible tokenizer directions and the choice is irreversible once pretraining starts: keep the existing 131k SPM-Unigram vocab (called the model's 'main remaining sovereign advantage'); retrain a new ~131-150k byte-level BPE on a code-heavy agentic mix; or adopt Qwen3's tokenizer outright to unlock drop-in logit distillation. These cannot all be satisfied, and the tokenizer cascades into distillation feasibility, code/JSON/shell inference cost, multilingual quality, and vLLM tool-call parser compatibility. Compounding this, the data plan devotes only ~5-10% of tokens to the sovereign languages that justify the entire 'sovereign model' identity, and no dimension quantified whether enough high-quality, commercially-licensable open data even exists in those languages or how much a 7B needs. Vocab allocation and language data mix must be decided together; getting either wrong either guts the differentiator or imposes a permanent per-token tax on the agentic workload.

## GAP: Whether Muon at 7B requires muP-style update scaling — reconciling the committed muon_disable_mup_scale=true decision with at-scale evidence and the Kimi K2 recipe
The project has ALREADY committed `muon_disable_mup_scale=true` to git ('Stage 2 optimizer locked'), but the optimizer dimension explicitly warns to keep muP-style update scaling ON for Muon at scale (citing a Dec-2025 transfer paper that Muon's advantage survives only with muP-scaled LR and width-scaled weight decay) and NOT to generalize a 250M-1B A/B that looked fine with scaling disabled. The same dimension also says to 'adopt the Kimi K2 configuration wholesale' — but whether K2 actually used muP width scaling (vs update-RMS matching as a substitute) is unclear, so the committed decision may contradict both the transfer paper and the very recipe being cloned. A wrong optimizer-scaling choice can silently degrade convergence or diverge the single most expensive and unrepeatable artifact (the ~6T-token, ~$500k main run). This must be resolved before committing compute, and the resolution is genuinely researchable from primary sources.
